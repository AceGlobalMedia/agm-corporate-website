The Process

AGM doesn’t begin with technology. AGM begins with the business.

Our methodology is the product. Nine steps that take a business from how it works today to a simplified, automated operation — with technology decided only after the operation is understood and redesigned.

DISCOVER→OBSERVE→MAP→IDENTIFY→DESIGN→BUILD→TEST→IMPLEMENT→OPTIMIZE

We don’t automate broken processes. We understand them first.

Most technology projects start with a solution: “we need an app,” “we need AI,” “we need a new website.” The result is often a faster version of the same problems.

Notice where technology appears in our process: step six. The first five steps happen inside your business — discovering, observing, mapping, identifying and designing. By the time we build, we know exactly what the operation needs.

The nine steps

DISCOVER → OBSERVE → MAP → IDENTIFY → DESIGN → BUILD → TEST → IMPLEMENT → OPTIMIZE

01

DISCOVER

Understand the business, its objectives, people, customers, systems and current challenges.

The question: “What is this business trying to achieve — and what is getting in the way?”

  • Leadership and stakeholder interviews
  • Business goals, growth plans and constraints
  • Current systems, tools and data inventory
  • Customer and staff pain points

Outcome: A shared understanding of the business and what success looks like.

02

OBSERVE

Learn how the work is actually being done — not merely how the process is supposed to work.

The question: “What really happens on a busy Tuesday afternoon?”

  • Time on the floor with the people doing the work
  • Shadowing front desk, warehouse, drivers, finance
  • Capturing workarounds, side-spreadsheets and chat threads
  • Noting where people wait, re-type or chase

Outcome: A true picture of the operation — including the parts nobody documented.

03

MAP

Map the complete workflow from beginning to end, including handoffs, exceptions, workarounds, approvals, communication and data movement.

The question: “Where does each piece of information start, travel and end?”

  • End-to-end workflow maps by department
  • Handoffs, approvals and decision points
  • Exceptions and edge cases
  • Data movement between people and systems

Outcome: A complete operational map everyone in the business recognises.

04

IDENTIFY

Find bottlenecks, duplicated effort, unnecessary manual work, disconnected systems, delays, risks and opportunities for automation or simplification.

The question: “What should be removed, simplified, connected or automated?”

  • Bottleneck and delay analysis
  • Duplicate entry and rework
  • Risk, error and control gaps
  • Prioritised opportunity list by impact

Outcome: A prioritised list of problems and opportunities, with the reasoning behind each.

05

DESIGN

Design the improved workflow first, then determine what technology is required to support it.

The question: “What should the operation look like — and only then, what does it need?”

  • Redesigned future-state workflow
  • Roles, permissions and responsibilities
  • Technology requirements derived from the workflow
  • Phased roadmap and scope

Outcome: An approved future-state operation and the technology blueprint to support it.

06

BUILD

Develop the appropriate solution — software, portals, websites, mobile experiences, dashboards, integrations, AI, automation, or a combination.

The question: “What is the right tool for each part of the improved workflow?”

  • Custom systems and portals
  • Web and mobile experiences
  • Dashboards and reporting
  • Integrations, AI and automation

Outcome: Working technology shaped around the redesigned operation.

07

TEST

Test against real-world workflows and edge cases with the people who will actually use the system.

The question: “Does it hold up on the busiest, messiest day?”

  • Scenario testing with real staff
  • Edge cases and exceptions from the map
  • Permissions and data accuracy checks
  • Feedback loops and refinements

Outcome: A system proven against real work, not ideal conditions.

08

IMPLEMENT

Introduce the solution into the business with the necessary configuration, migration, training and operational transition.

The question: “How do we change the way people work without disrupting the business?”

  • Configuration and data migration
  • Role-based staff training
  • Phased rollout and parallel running
  • Go-live support on the floor

Outcome: The business running on the new operation, with staff confident using it.

09

OPTIMIZE

Continue measuring, refining and improving the system as the business evolves.

The question: “What have we learned — and what should improve next?”

  • Usage and performance measurement
  • Operational KPI tracking
  • Continuous refinements
  • New phases as the business grows

Outcome: An operation that keeps getting better, not a project that ends at launch.

How you work today
